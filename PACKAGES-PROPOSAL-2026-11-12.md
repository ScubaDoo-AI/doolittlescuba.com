## How to build it — two routes

### Route A — one automatic discount (recommended first; no app, nothing to fulfil differently)
Shopify → Discounts → *Buy X get Y*, automatic:
- Customer buys **1 from Site Caps or Brand Caps** (both collections in one condition: 492329042106, 492329074874)
- Customer gets **1 from Signature Series** (492282708154) at **$8 off**
- no per-order maximum (two caps + two tees = two pairs = $16 off); combines with nothing else.

Mix and match is free with this: any cap, any tee, light or dark, and the buyer never sees a bundle product — just two ordinary line items with the discount applied, which is exactly what Printful and the embroiderer already fulfil. The matching set is merchandising, not mechanics: the cap page says *"Pair it with the Black Bart tee — $8 off at checkout"*, the tee page says the reverse, the home page hats deck and each site guide get an **"Add the pair"** button that drops both into the cart (a cart permalink with the two variants; the Make-it-yours page already knows the site and colour and can take the two sizes). Cost: nothing. Time: an hour for the discount and copy; a short pass for the buttons.

### Route B — set products (one page per site)
Fourteen "**<Site> — the pair**" products, each with a combined mockup (cap beside the tee — the image at the end of this note is the shape), options *Cap size* (S/M, L/XL) · *Tee* (light, dark) · *Tee size* (S–3XL) = 24 variants each, priced $69 / $72, inventory following the components. Two ways to make them:
- **Shopify Bundles** (Shopify's own app, free): fixed bundles. Orders carry the component line items with a reference to the parent, which is what a fulfilment app needs — but Printful does not document Shopify's native bundles, only *Simple Bundles* and *Bold Bundles*. One test order would settle it.
- **Simple Bundles & Kits** (Printful's documented path): free for 3 bundles and 50 orders a month, $24/month beyond that; its "infinite options" bundle also does true mix and match on one page (pick cap, pick tee, pick sizes) without a variant explosion.

Route B gives a set its own page, photo and share link, and lets the pair sit in collections and on the home page as a thing of its own. It costs an app, a test order, 14 more products to keep in step every time a cap or tee changes, and — on Simple Bundles — $24 a month once there are more than three. My recommendation: **A now, and B only if pairs sell and deserve their own pages**, after the sample cap is in hand and a test order has gone through whichever bundle app is chosen.
